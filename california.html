How This California Paycheck Calculator Works

This calculator estimates your take-home pay in California by subtracting federal income tax, California state income tax, Social Security, and Medicare from your gross salary. California uses a progressive tax system, meaning higher earners pay a larger percentage. Additionally, most employees in California are subject to State Disability Insurance (SDI), which is an extra mandatory deduction unique to the state.

Planning to relocate? Compare your take-home pay with the Texas Paycheck Calculator (0% state tax) or the New York Paycheck Calculator to see the difference.

California vs. Neighboring States (No State Tax)

How does California compare? California has one of the highest state income tax rates in the U.S., with a top marginal rate of 13.3%. In contrast, neighboring Nevada has 0% state income tax, and Arizona has a flat 2.5% rate. If you are considering a move for work, your take-home pay on the exact same salary will be significantly higher in Nevada or Texas compared to California.

California Paycheck Calculator FAQ

Does California have state income tax?

Yes. California has a progressive state income tax system with rates ranging from 1% to 13.3%, depending on your income level and filing status.

What is SDI in California?

SDI stands for State Disability Insurance. It is a mandatory payroll deduction in California (usually around 1.1% of your wages) that provides short-term benefits if you are unable to work due to non-work-related illness or injury. This calculator factors this into the overall state tax estimate.

Is California a high-tax state?

Yes. California consistently ranks among the states with the highest income tax burdens in the country. That is why comparing your take-home pay with states like Texas or Nevada is highly recommended before accepting a job offer.
